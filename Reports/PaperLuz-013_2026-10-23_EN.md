## 1. Executive Summary & Key Strategic Insights

1. **North American Packaging Giant PCA (PKG) Posts Q3 Earnings Beat, Validating $840/ST Peak Raw Spread Conversion to Massive Operating Cash Flow**: Packaging Corporation of America reported Q3 2026 earnings on October 22, delivering net sales of $2.18B and EPS well ahead of consensus estimates. The report demonstrated that PCA's industry-leading $140/ST price hike initiated September 1 was fully accepted by downstream accounts, propelling packaging margin expansion. Corrugated product shipments climbed +5.2% YoY with mill operating rates reaching a robust 93%, proving that North America's record $840/ST raw material spread ($975 Linerboard vs. $136 OCC) has translated into substantial real-economy cash flow.
2. **EU PFHxA Ban Enters Mandatory Enforcement on October 18; Global Food Packaging Enters "Zero-PFAS Regulatory Era"**: Regulation (EU) 2024/2462 restricting undecafluorohexanoic acid (PFHxA), its salts, and related substances in food contact paper and board crossed its final transition threshold on October 18, 2026. European border customs are conducting rigorous total fluorine testing (25 ppb limit / 1,000 ppb related substances), barring non-compliant hamburger wraps, bakery bags, and pizza boxes. Chung Hwa Pulp's (CHP 1905) CircuWell aqueous dispersion barrier board and Taiwanese converting clusters are experiencing a surge in European export orders.
3. **Surging Paper & Pulp Prices Drive CHP (1905) Stock Rally as Taiwan Big 4 Mobilize for Peak Q4 E-Commerce Demand**: According to the *Liberty Times* on October 24, Chung Hwa Pulp shares surged with heavy trading volume as fourth-quarter finished paper and virgin pulp prices strengthened simultaneously, coupled with accelerating European exports of CircuWell food-safe barrier board. As forecasted during CHP's institutional investor conference, September's +$20/MT hardwood pulp hike has begun transmitting into cultural and specialty paper prices after a standard 1–2 month lag. Joined by Cheng Loong (NT$4.178B), Long Chen (NT$4.790B, 9M pre-tax EPS NT$0.46), and YFY (NT$6.653B), Taiwanese paper leaders are running full out for Double 11 and year-end inventory replenishment.
4. **China Containerboard Two-Wave Hikes (+RMB 100/MT Total) Fully Implemented; Box Plant Restocking Lifts Domestic OCC**: Following early-October adjustments, Chinese containerboard leaders led by Nine Dragons and Shanying implemented their second +RMB 50/MT price increase around October 20, bringing cumulative October gains to +RMB 100/MT. Taiwanese mills in China (Long Chen and YFY) followed suit. Supported by Double 11 demand and disciplined mill supply, boxmakers transitioned from hesitation to aggressive restocking, nudging Grade-A domestic OCC mill-gate benchmarks up to RMB 2,045/MT (USD 288/MT).
5. **Taiwan Specialty Paper Innovation Enters Global Luxury Fashion: Historic Puli Mill Supplies Abaca Paper Yarn to Balenciaga**: Demonstrating high-value transformation beyond commodity paper, a three-generation heritage paper mill in Puli, Nantou, engineered high-tensile ultra-thin paper from Manila hemp (abaca) fibers, slitting and twisting it into "Paper Yarn." The bio-based textile was successfully adopted by French luxury house Balenciaga for $1,000+ designer denim and high-end Italian apparel, providing a blueprint for paper manufacturers seeking high-margin niches away from commodity price wars.
6. **Imported Virgin Pulp Prices Step Up Firmly as Chinese Port Stocks Drop for 15th Consecutive Week to 2.040M MT**: China imported softwood NBSK spot edged up to USD 695/MT (RMB 4,940/MT), while imported hardwood BHKP spot firmed at USD 629/MT (RMB 4,475/MT), keeping the NBSK-BHKP spread steady at RMB 465/MT (USD 66/MT). Sample pulp inventories at Qingdao and Changshu ports fell for the 15th straight week to 2.040M MT (-11.4% from July peaks), evidencing healthy downstream paper mill consumption during the autumn peak season.
7. **Suzano October Price Hikes Fully Accepted as UPM Curtails Pietarsaari Mill to Discipline Supply**: Suzano's October price hikes (+US$50/MT in Europe/Americas to ~$1,360/MT; +US$20/MT in Asia) have been completely realized in commercial billings. Concurrently, UPM's planned maintenance downtime and temporary curtailments at its Pietarsaari pulp mill in Finland effectively alleviated Nordic roundwood cost inflation and supported hardwood pulp market confidence, while Arauco's 3.5M MT Sucuriú megaproject in Brazil advanced ahead of schedule to 82% completion.
8. **Brent Crude Consolidates Around $74/bbl, Lowering Chemical Sizing, Latex, and Logistics Costs**: Following sharp two-week declines, Brent crude settled near $74.30/bbl (with WTI at $70.50/bbl). Softer petroleum prices triggered a 4–7% cost decline in essential papermaking chemicals (AKD sizing, SBR latex, PE coating resins) and reduced maritime bunker surcharges. In tandem, Taiwanese mills increased solid recovered fuel (SRF) co-firing substitution to 49.0%, holding industrial steam costs at a low NT$718/ton.
9. **Global Container Freight Index (SCFI) Moderates to 3,325 Points with Plentiful Export Vessel Capacity**: The Shanghai Containerized Freight Index (SCFI) eased 0.6% WoW to 3,325.40 points (US West Coast $6,420/FEU, US East Coast $9,210/FEU), while the Baltic Dry Index (BDI) slipped to 1,840 points. Transpacific and Europe-bound container capacity remained orderly and congestion-free, ensuring unobstructed holiday deliveries for Taiwanese specialty papers and Cheng Loong's Vietnam operations.
10. **Firm US Dollar (USD/TWD 32.120) Maintains Stable Import Costs and European Export Competitiveness**: The New Taiwan Dollar closed at 32.120 per USD (easing 4 cents WoW), while USD/CNY held at 7.1250 and EUR/USD softened to 1.0850. Currency stability keeps imported OCC and pulp procurement costs within budgeted ranges. Meanwhile, sustained European energy and operational expenses preserve a 15–20% pricing edge for Taiwanese PFAS-free food boards entering European distribution.

## 4. Papermaking Raw Materials: Pulp & Recovered Paper

The global woodpulp market exhibited a disciplined environment characterized by the full implementation of producer price increases and accelerating inventory drawdowns. China imported Northern Bleached Softwood Kraft (NBSK) spot edged up to USD 695/MT (RMB 4,940/MT), while Bleached Hardwood Kraft Pulp (BHKP) spot stabilized at USD 629/MT (RMB 4,475/MT). On the Shanghai Futures Exchange (SHFE), the active softwood pulp futures contract settled at RMB 4,878/MT (USD 685/MT), maintaining a balanced spot-futures basis of +RMB 62/MT (+USD 8/MT). Inventory dynamics were notably constructive: combined sample inventories at Qingdao and Changshu ports decreased for the 15th consecutive week to 2.040M MT (-12k MT WoW), marking a cumulative drop of over 260,000 MT from July peaks as cultural and tissue mills steadily took deliveries for holiday packaging runs.

On the supply front, market leaders demonstrated robust pricing discipline. First, **Suzano's** announced October global price hikes (+US$50/MT in Europe and North America to ~$1,360/MT; +US$20/MT in Asia) were fully accepted by international buyers and converted to commercial billings. Second, **UPM's** maintenance downtime and temporary production curtailments at its Pietarsaari pulp mill in Finland effectively mitigated Nordic roundwood cost inflation. Third, Chile's **Arauco** reported that its $4.6B **Sucuriú megaproject in Brazil (3.5M MT/yr hardwood pulp capacity) reached 82% completion**, entering an intensive mechanical and electrical installation phase on track for a late-2027 startup.

In recovered fiber markets, US 11# OCC held steady at USD 135–137/ST FAS on the West Coast and USD 137–139/ST FAS on the East Coast. In mainland China, the full implementation of two consecutive waves of containerboard price hikes (+RMB 100/MT cumulative) stimulated box plant restocking, prompting domestic Grade-A OCC mill-gate prices to edge up RMB 10/MT to RMB 2,045/MT (USD 288/MT). In Taiwan, mill-gate scrap paper prices remained unchanged at NT$4,200/MT (USD 130.8/MT), supporting high operating rates across the Big 4.
